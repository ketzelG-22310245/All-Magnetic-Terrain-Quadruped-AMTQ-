# Overview

**AMTQ (All Magnetic Terrain Quadruped)** is a multidisciplinary robotics project focused on the design, development, and experimental validation of a magnetic quadruped robotic platform intended for future industrial maintenance and inspection applications on ferromagnetic structures.

The project integrates several engineering disciplines into a single robotic system, including:

- Mechanical Design
- Embedded Electronics
- Firmware Development
- Control Systems
- Robot Kinematics
- MATLAB & Simulink
- ROS2 Integration
- Experimental Validation

Rather than remaining a simulation-only academic project, AMTQ has been physically built and validated through multiple development stages, including prototype manufacturing, electronics integration, locomotion testing, magnetic adhesion experiments, and iterative mechanical redesign.

The repository documents the complete engineering process behind the project, from the initial concept to the current AMTQ V2 architecture.

---

# The Problem

Large ferromagnetic structures such as storage tanks, bridges, offshore platforms, industrial facilities, and steel infrastructure require periodic inspection and maintenance to ensure structural integrity and operational safety.

Traditional maintenance methods frequently involve:

- Rope access
- Scaffolding
- Elevated work platforms
- Direct human intervention

These approaches increase operational costs, inspection time, and worker exposure to hazardous environments.

Developing robotic platforms capable of safely traversing steel surfaces has become an active area of research due to their potential to improve safety, reduce operational costs, and enable future autonomous maintenance systems.

---

# Engineering Objectives

The primary objectives of AMTQ are:

- Design a modular quadruped robotic platform.
- Develop a magnetic adhesion system for steel surfaces.
- Design custom embedded electronics.
- Implement robot kinematics using MATLAB.
- Validate locomotion through physical experiments.
- Integrate ROS2 for future autonomous operation.
- Develop an expandable research platform for industrial robotics.

---

# The Solution

AMTQ addresses these challenges through a magnetic quadruped robotic platform capable of generating stable locomotion while maintaining magnetic adhesion on ferromagnetic surfaces.

The system combines:

- Mechanical engineering
- Embedded systems
- Custom electronics
- Robot control
- Inverse kinematics
- MATLAB simulation
- ROS2 integration
- Modular architecture

The platform has evolved through multiple iterations, progressively improving locomotion performance, structural rigidity, electronic integration, and software architecture.
